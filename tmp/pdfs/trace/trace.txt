Where Trace stands
Trace started with a simple hypothesis: people have everyday stories to share, but traditional social platforms make it hard to post them.
Early usage validated part of this. People wrote personal stories, responded to topics, continued narratives, and engaged in replies —
showing that topics can lower the barrier to expression and that everyday experiences can become meaningful content.
That showed topics could successfully lower the barrier to expression, and that everyday experiences were interesting enough to become
social content.
But a second problem emerged. Getting someone to write once is very different from getting them to return.
While users were willing to explore and participate, retention was weak. I had initially centered the product around creation, but behaviour
showed a stronger need for discovery before participation. This shifted Trace’s direction toward prioritising interesting stories, people, and
narrative continuity before creation, while keeping writing available when users are ready.
So the problem evolved from: “How do I make everyday stories easier to share?”

to:

“How do I make them worth coming back for?”


That is what I am now validating.
